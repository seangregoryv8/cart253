# Stardrops by Leah
### https://leahiscoding.github.io/CART-253/art-jam/

Most of what I found interesting about this one was the abstract definition of a self-portrait. After talking to her personally and hearing how she described how she saw herself as "bright" and "starry", it struck me how nice beginning to learn how to code is. It's always nice to see a project, despite its shortcomings and flaws, think outside the box and actually showcase something no one has thought of before. A few interesting notes are how it can't be infinitely run, as eventually the stars will get so fast that the program crashes. It shows room for improvement in the project, showing a sense of development. One thing I found quite interesting in this was knowing the level of programming Leah is at, and seeing her figure out interesting portions of this, such as randomly-generated colours and randomly-generated fall speeds to provide an almost-third dimension to the project. Whilst the project isnt well optimized, no project from a new programmer ever is. Some other parts I do find interesting would be the shine of the stars, its a nice touch, and with the black background, it makes it feel like a proper night sky. A few suggestions I may have for her would be, besides fixing the crashing bug after having it on for so long, to have the stars rotate and twirl around a bit, allowing for some more variety. If she's going for interactability, I'd recommend being able to catch the stars in some way, or even summon your own stars, kinda like shooting stars.
<br><br>There's a wide variety of things that she can do, but of those that I found most interesting was the abstract work she put into the project. Most went for normal face-like things, even I did the same, but she knew her limitations and worked alongside them, which I have a lot of respect for. There isn't too much else to discuss with her project, and the code cannot be viewed, though I can piece together how it responds and looks. I have no comment upon the code clarity and structure, but almost seem to find the stars moving around quite hypnotizing to watch. I can actively feel myself getting tired watching them, in a good way of doing so.

# Ari and Soup
### https://wonarii.github.io/cart253/art-jam

I really love the art in this project. I initially thought it was a simple self-portrait, which incorporates a good amount of complex shapes to make the hair and even glasses. However, I couldn't help but notice the soup, given how it emitted ovals around it, akin to steam. I reached for the soup bowl and it was actually interactible. Seems as though the soup itself has a designated spot, one that even lights up when you grab it or are about to place it back down. I initially thought I could feed the soup to Ari, but then, as I placed the bowl close to her and repeatedly clicked, nothing happened. I initially thought the project was incomplete, until I realized that I was actually fogging up her glasses. That was such an interesting detail, since I know, as a fellow glasses user, how annoying fogged up glasses can be. Whenever I cook, I always forget that I need to stand away when I open the oven, as it fogs up my glasses immediately, likewise when I finish a long day outside in the frozen cold and the humidity hitting my face fogs them up as well. However, another thing I realized from the portrait was that the individual lenses themselves fogged up depending on where I positioned the soup, meaning that she must have used some form of hitbox detection to figure out where her glasses were and how to fog them up. Initially I thought that, if I fully fogged up both lenses, something would happen, but alas, nothing occured. One final portion I found interesting was that they defogged after some time once the soup was moved away.
<br><br>A little experiment I ran was keeping the soup on her glasses for a long while and seeing if they had some sort of timer attached, and, it turns out, they do. It likely seems to be a continuous tick per frame depending on if the soup is located in the correct hitbox, where it decreases further and further as the soup is away. However, whilst it has a minimum size of likely 0 for the opacity of the lens, it doesn't seem to have a maximum from the experiment I ran, or it has but the maximum is set to a high level. If I put the soup to her lens and move it as soon as the lens fully fogs, it begins to unfog immediately. However, if I move it there and keep it there for a while, it takes much longer to defog, suggesting no capped maximum. I cannot tell if that's an oversight, but I'll assume it isn't. One thing I may suggest doing is to maybe add a "surprised" face to her once both glasses have been fogged up, maybe as a little indication that things worked. It could add for some fun variety to the portrait and be something quite intriguing to see.
