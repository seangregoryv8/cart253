# Fly Demise by Lazor
### https://lazer6769.github.io/Cart-253/Topics/frogmodjam/frogfrogfrog/

I really do love a game that can make me chuckle, even if done ironically. This game has it all when it comes to its meme strategy, by having Pepe all over the place, alongside the comically used soundtrack and sound effects that repeat endlessly. While it isn't for everyone, this project is really funny to me.

With this game, it sends you to hell, and demands that you eat 30 flies (10 of each kind) to survive. Why? Why not.

The gameplay is quite fast-paced, and it somehow feels like I'm losing and winning at the exact same time. It makes the project that much funnier in the process, especially with all the sound effects going all at once. The image used for the background is comedically-accurate hell, and the win screen has a variety of the ironic motifs, from the lack of capitalization to the Family Guy devil costume to misspelling, it all works together to form a really funny comedic masterpiece that only lasts a few minutes and does great work with the base frog template. My critiques for the game would likely be to have a lose screen (unless I never got to it), alongside some more things for the frog itself rather than just the base frog model. Other than that, the game itself is quite addictive to play, if only there were some sort of infinite mode for you to be able to play for a high score.

# Poison Fly Game by Amelie Barrette
### https://nounoune666.github.io/cart253/topics/Mod_Jam/

On the other side is a more cute game of a flying frog, quite a twist to mix them both together. When I loaded the game up, I was presented with "Play" and "Instructions", which was quite odd. I was expecting some kind of title, even if it was just a base text element. Whilst I like the menu system, it felt off to me, since I initially didn't think of it as a menu system.
<br><br>
When I booted the game up, it started right away without leeway. I noted that the frog was, indeed, quite cute, and the flies came at me right away in quite a big wall, noting of the poisonous red flies that the instructions had taught me about. However, I inevitably touched one of the red flies, and got a game over with a nice little jingle that played when I died, and a cute face with "You ate a poisonous fly!". I understood that the game itself was going for the pocket flash game sort of atmosphere and aesthetic, which fits the style and made for a pleasant gameplay experience. I just wish that the poisonous fly was not an instant game over.

Another big thing I really liked was the implementation of the keys that moved you between the "title" screen, instructions screen, play screen and game over screen. It made it quite approachable and everything was implemented quite clearly.

However, pleasantly enough, the more I played, the more the night shifted, which made the sky a pleasant deep blue. Finally, when I went through enough, I got a very nice victory screen with another emoticon and a congrats, alongside showing the amount of flies I had eaten throughout the night.

The game overall felt comforting to play, and the sprites and music were implemented nicely. Alongside the critiques I provided above, I believe something else that could be made is for you to actively control the frog with a variety of fashions. I use a trackpad for my stuff, and playing for a little while gets a bit crampy. 

# Fight Fight Fight by Sama 
### https://consumedlocusts.github.io/cart253/topics/frog.mod.jam/

Going into this game, I expected another frog-type game, but was pleasantly surprised to see a Galaga-like shooter with Cruelty Squad like artstyle and elements associated with it. You, interestingly enough, begin by scanning the playing field for enemies, and once you spot it, 8 of them spawn. You have a reticle on your ship that, when mouse1 is held, shoots continuously, only stopping when it hits an enemy. It's a very unique way of shooting that's done here. Once all 8 have been dispatched, a boss arrives, and attacks you whilst you wittle down his health.

Unfortunately, the game falls apart from there, as his health moves to the negatives when you are meant to defeat him. I would've loved to see how the ending went, but cannot get to it. If the ending is actively in there, there's a major bug that affected all 5 of my playthroughs that doesn't let me kill the boss. If there isn't an ending, that's a problem, since the boss still continues to hit you. However, I should say "tries to hit", since the boss cannot aim his shots at you, always missing. Since you cannot move at all (I mashed my keyboard to test), you're stuck waiting for when the boss decides to finally hit you. It all feels clunky and off to have a game where you cannot kill the boss and the boss cannot kill you.

However, the artstyle and graphics of the game are so beautifully disgusting that it draws me in, and equally frustrates me of the lackluster gameplay. The interesting aiming mechanic would've worked really well if you were able to move at all, and the "scanning" system is awesome to see, if it was used more than once at the beginning and was more than just "click where the enemy is".

On that note, the invisible enemies during the scan that slowly appear in provides a moment of genuinely intriguing design, since it's just you and the terrifyingly good enemy design.

Everything about this game feels like a contradiction, and I really hope it gets expanded on even more, maybe in something deeper in the Variation Jam.
